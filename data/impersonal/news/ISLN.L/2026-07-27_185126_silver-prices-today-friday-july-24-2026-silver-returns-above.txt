Silver futures opened at $57.92 per ounce on Friday, July 24, 2026 and moved higher to $58.87. On July 21 silver stood at $59.12 per ounce, more than $51 above one year ago. Strength in the U.S. dollar contributed to recent declines, and inflation risk continues to limit recovery; since silver pays no interest, investors shift into yield-bearing assets when rates rise. A mix of constrained supply and rising industrial demand plus investor demand has pushed prices higher this year, and the Silver Institute's World Silver Survey 2026 confirmed a sixth consecutive annual supply deficit at 46.3 million ounces. Per CME FedWatch the consensus for the next rate decision is a hold, but that probability declined to 64.2% from 87.2%.
