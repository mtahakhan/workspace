Gold prices were little changed at around $4,050 per ounce on Friday, steadying after a nearly 2% drop in the previous session (late July 2026).

Gold traded near $4,038 per ounce as of July 13, 2026 - roughly 28% below its January all-time high of $5,595, a significant pullback from record highs earlier in the year.

Factors in play this month:
1. Middle East tensions - gold struggled in recent weeks amid escalating hostilities, with supply disruption spreading from the Strait of Hormuz to the Red Sea.
2. Interest rate expectations - investors await next week's Federal Reserve meeting, where rates are widely expected to remain unchanged, though markets price roughly an 80% chance of a hike in September.
3. Oil price impact - higher oil prices driven by Gulf supply concerns have reinforced expectations that rates stay higher for longer, reducing the appeal of non-yielding gold.

Long-term analyst outlook remains bullish despite the mid-year pullback: JP Morgan predicts $6,300 by end-2026; Deutsche Bank sees above $6,000; UBS and Societe Generale predict $6,200 and $6,000 respectively.
