Gold trades near $4,038-4,050/oz in late July 2026, roughly 28% below its January all-time high of $5,595. The decline is attributed to hawkish Fed expectations - markets price ~80% odds of a September hike - while higher oil prices from Gulf supply disruption reinforce higher-for-longer rate expectations, reducing the appeal of non-yielding gold. The structural drivers behind the January record (central bank buying, fiscal expansion, reserve diversification) have not reversed. Bank forecasts remain well above spot: JP Morgan $6,300 by end-2026, Deutsche Bank above $6,000, UBS $6,200, Société Générale $6,000. Relevant to the portfolio's largest position: the drawdown is a rate-driven correction within an intact structural case, not a broken thesis - but it does not change the fact that the hedge sleeve is over its cap.
