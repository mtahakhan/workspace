IREN's multi-week slide is attributed to a fiscal Q3 revenue miss ($144.8 million versus roughly $219.87 million consensus, a shortfall of more than 34%, which management blamed on lower Bitcoin prices and decommissioning older mining hardware for GPU installs), a $2 billion convertible notes offering, tabled Oklahoma tax incentives, and a governance dispute over an unusually large restricted stock unit grant of roughly $700-800 million to its co-CEOs. Competitive pressure increased as Meta Platforms shifted from purely consuming cloud compute to reselling excess AI capacity. Shares fell from $66.60 on June 2 to $38.83 by July 2, about -41.7%. Context: combined with the July 20 $2.8bn contract release already stored, the picture is a sentiment/dilution de-rating against improving contracted revenue - the tactical catalyst remains live.
